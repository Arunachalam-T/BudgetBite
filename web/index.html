Undergraduate DAA Algorithm Documentation

1. Dynamic Programming Formulation (Multi-Choice Knapsack Variant)

The meal planning problem is modelled as a constrained optimization problem analogous to the 0-1 Knapsack / Multiple-Choice Knapsack problem. We discretize the total weekly budget \(B\) into \(W = 1000\) discrete cost quanta. There are \(D = 7\) days and \(S = 3\) meal slots per day (total \(21\) decisions).

State Definition: dp[s][j] = Maximum cumulative nutritional value achievable considering meal slots 0 .. s using at most j budget units (where j ∈ [0, W]). Value Metric: value(r) = 0.5 * (kcal_r / target_kcal_slot) + 0.5 * (protein_r / target_protein_slot) Recurrence Relation: For each slot s from 0 to 20: For each candidate recipe r eligible for slot (s mod 3): cost_u = round(effective_cost(r) * W / weekly_budget) dp[j] = max( dp[j], dp_prev[j - cost_u] + value(r) ) for j = W down to cost_u Backtracking / Reconstruction: choice[s][j] stores the candidate index selected to produce the optimal transition, allowing an exact O(D * S) traceback path.
Time & Space Complexity: DP
  • Time Complexity: O(D × S × R × W) where \(D=7\), \(S=3\), \(R \approx 350\) candidate recipes, and \(W=1000\). Total operations: ≈ 7.3 × 106 (computes in < 50 ms in C++17).
  • Space Complexity: O(W) for 1-D rolling DP state, and O(D × S × W) for the choice reconstruction matrix.
Time & Space Complexity: Greedy Baseline
  • Time Complexity: O(D × S × R × log R) to sort recipes by density ratio \(\frac{\text{value}(r)}{\text{cost}(r)}\).
  • Space Complexity: O(R) auxiliary space for candidate pointers.
  • Theoretical Flaw of Greedy: Because the knapsack decision is discrete (cannot take fractional portions of a recipe), Greedy makes locally optimal choices that can prematurely exhaust the budget or leave leftover budget that could have unlocked significantly superior options.
